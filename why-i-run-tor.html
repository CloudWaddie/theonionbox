Why I run Tor

I operate a Tor exit relay and some other relays and bridges. Here's why.

Privacy is a basic right

Everyone should be able to read, write and look things up without being tracked, profiled or censored. Tor makes that possible, and it only works because ordinary people donate bandwidth to carry it. I have the bandwidth and the skills, so I run relays.

Who Tor helps

Journalists and their sources, activists under repressive governments, people escaping abusive situations, people reading about sensitive medical topics, and anyone who just doesn't want to be followed around the web. The more relays there are, the harder it is to block or surveil the network.

Why an exit, specifically

Exits are the scarcest and most legally awkward part of the network, so fewer people run them. That makes them the part Tor needs most. Running one means I sometimes get abuse complaints about traffic I don't control, much like a phone company isn't responsible for what people say on a call. If you have a complaint, please read the notice on my home page and EFF's Tor legal FAQ first.

Bridges

Bridges are unlisted entry points for people whose networks block Tor. They let someone in a censored country reach the open internet.
